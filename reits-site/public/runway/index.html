REIT Runway

Capital sourcing for JV equity, lenders and bankers: which public REITs need capital in the next 24 months, read from their SEC filings.

Filings: SEC 10-K and 10-Q Rates: — Research write-up

What this is. REIT Runway reads the latest 10-K and 10-Q of every listed U.S. equity REIT (146 companies) from SEC EDGAR and asks one question: how much debt comes due in the next 24 months, and can the company pay it from cash, retained cash flow and its credit line? Whatever is left has to come from outside, through a bond deal, a secured loan, preferred or JV equity, or asset sales.

Where the maturity schedule comes from. Companies report it three ways, so each 10-K is read in this order: the standard XBRL maturity tags, the same tags split by debt type, and finally the maturity table printed in the debt footnote. Each result is checked against the company's reported total debt, and the closest match is kept. When no table parses cleanly, the schedule was read by hand from the same 10-K. Every company shows which source was used, with a link to the filing.

  • Funding sources: cash on the latest balance sheet, retained cash flow (operating cash flow less dividends over the last 12 months, scaled to the window) and undrawn revolver capacity where the filing tags it.
  • Outcomes: Self-funded (cash and retained cash flow cover what's due) · Revolver covers it · Routine refinance (leverage at or under 50% of book assets, or interest covered 3.5× or more) · Needs new capital · Stretched (dividend not covered by cash flow, interest covered less than 1.75×, or very high leverage) · Insufficient data.
  • Capital need score (0 to 100) weights the size of what's due at 35%, the share that has to come from outside at 35%, and stress at 30% (leverage, dividend coverage and the rate increase on refinancing).
  • Limits: leverage is debt over book value (real estate at cost, or total assets). For REITs that bought their properties long ago, book value is well below market value, so leverage can look higher than it is. The schedule is as of the last 10-K, so some of this year's maturities may already be refinanced. Revolver availability is often stated only in MD&A text, which isn't in the XBRL data; those companies are flagged.
