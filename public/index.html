Executive summary

Refi Radar is a deal-sourcing tool for commercial real estate debt. It reads the loan-level data that public CMBS trusts file with the SEC each month and finds the 472 loans from 2016 and 2017 deals that come due by the end of 2027, $22.6B across 129 metro areas. Those loans were written at a median coupon of 4.7% and would price near 6.9% today. The tool sizes each refinance against five lender types at current spreads and ranks the loans by how soon they mature, how large they are and whether the numbers can work.

For originators

Banks, life companies, debt funds and CMBS lenders

Find maturing loans that fit your lending criteria before they come to market. Filter by MSA, property type, size and maturity date, then enter your own spreads and leverage limits. Each loan shows whether your program would pay off the existing debt, which test limits the loan (LTV, DSCR or debt yield), and how much mezz or preferred equity the borrower would still need.

For brokers

Debt and equity capital advisors

Find borrowers who will need a refinance, a mezz or preferred equity recap, or a workout in the next 15 months. Each loan comes with a lender-by-lender comparison, a proposed capital stack and pitch notes for the first call. The 11 largest targets also have Claude-written briefs that name the sponsor from the deal prospectus.

At today's rates, only 13% of this balance can refinance at the same size with senior debt alone. The rest will need subordinate capital, new equity or a negotiated extension, and each of those is a financing assignment.

Grouped by Census metro area (MSA), most of the balance sits in large office and mixed-use loans in gateway cities. The model applies its highest cap rates to office, so those loans show the largest refinancing gaps. Each bar splits a metro's balance by outcome at today's rates.

What binds

Property value sets how much most of these loans can borrow.

Each refinance is sized three ways: loan-to-value, debt service coverage and debt yield. At today's spreads the loan-to-value test is the lowest of the three for 93% of the balance, so the appraised value decides the loan amount more often than the new interest rate does. How it's built

From SEC filings to a call list

I built Refi Radar in a day with Claude. The loan data is free, because every public CMBS trust files a loan-level tape with the SEC each month. Most of the work went into cleaning those tapes and sizing each loan the way a lender would.

1

Pull the tapes

Form ABS-EE (Ex-102) for 15 conduit deals issued between December 2016 and November 2017, refreshed whenever a trust files its monthly tape.

2

Clean and map

Merge split notes and pari passu pieces, drop defeased loans and assign each property to its Census MSA, which leaves 472 loans.

3

Size the refinance

Test each loan against five lender programs on LTV, DSCR and debt yield. Take the best execution, then fill any gap with mezz and pref.

4

Find the sponsor

The tapes don't name borrowers, so Claude reads each deal's prospectus and pulls the sponsor with a citation.

Claude
5

Write the pitch

Every loan gets pitch notes from its numbers. The 11 largest targets also have briefs written by Claude.

Claude

Fixed: partial-year financials

The General Motors Building first showed $48M of cash flow against $222M underwritten, because the servicer had reported a single quarter. 303 loans report partial periods, and the tool now annualizes all of them.

Estimated: pari passu balances

A trust often holds one piece of a larger loan, so the tool estimates the whole-loan balance from reported debt service. For the GM Building it estimates $2.30B, which matches the $2.3B loan.

Missed: 3 of 14 sponsors

The prospectus search found sponsors for 11 of 14 target loans. The other three weren't described in the prospectuses I pulled, so those loans are marked for a manual check and no sponsor is shown.
